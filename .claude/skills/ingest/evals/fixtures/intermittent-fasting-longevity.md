# Intermittent Fasting and Longevity: What the Latest Research Says

Intermittent fasting (IF) has emerged as one of the most studied dietary interventions for extending healthspan. This article reviews the latest findings from clinical trials and mechanistic studies.

## Key Research Findings

A 2025 randomized controlled trial published in *Cell Metabolism* followed 250 participants over 12 months. The time-restricted eating (TRE) group (eating within an 8-hour window) showed:

- ==14% reduction in fasting insulin levels== compared to control
- 8% improvement in insulin sensitivity (HOMA-IR)
- Significant upregulation of autophagy markers (LC3-II/I ratio increased 2.3x)

> [!annotation] 這個研究很重要，因為它是目前最大規模的 TRE RCT
> 而且追蹤時間夠長（12個月）

## Molecular Mechanisms

The benefits of IF appear to work through several interconnected pathways:

1. **mTOR inhibition**: Fasting periods suppress mTOR signaling, which normally promotes cell growth. This shift allows cells to enter a repair and recycling mode.

2. **AMPK activation**: Energy depletion during fasting activates AMP-activated protein kinase, a master regulator of cellular energy homeostasis.

3. **Autophagy**: ==The most exciting mechanism — cells literally eat their own damaged components during extended fasting, replacing them with fresh ones.==

4. **Sirtuins**: NAD+ levels rise during fasting, activating sirtuin proteins (especially SIRT1 and SIRT3) that regulate aging-related processes.

## The Longo Fasting-Mimicking Diet (FMD)

Dr. Valter Longo's lab at USC has developed a 5-day fasting-mimicking diet that provides ~800 calories/day with specific macronutrient ratios. Key findings:

- 3 cycles of FMD over 3 months reduced biological age markers by an average of 2.5 years
- Participants showed reduced IGF-1 levels (a key aging biomarker)
- The protocol is easier to adhere to than water fasting while providing similar benefits

## Controversies

- **Muscle mass concerns**: Some researchers, notably Dr. Stuart Phillips, argue that IF may compromise muscle protein synthesis in older adults
- **Gender differences**: Women may respond differently to extended fasting protocols, with some studies showing disrupted hormonal patterns
- **Eating disorder risk**: The structured eating windows can trigger or worsen disordered eating in susceptible individuals

## Practical Recommendations

For longevity-focused individuals:
1. Start with 16:8 TRE (16 hours fasting, 8 hours eating)
2. Consider 2-3 FMD cycles per year for deeper cellular rejuvenation
3. Prioritize protein intake during eating windows (1.6g/kg/day minimum)
4. Monitor blood biomarkers: fasting glucose, insulin, IGF-1, inflammatory markers
